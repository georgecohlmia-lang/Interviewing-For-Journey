The ask · fifteen minutes, one question, a named day

Say what it’s for and say you won’t sell them anything. People say no to “do you have half an hour for some feedback,” because it sounds like work. Don’t send a survey: a survey gets you ratings, and you need the story.

The standard ask

The ask for the one who walked away

The hardest ask and the most valuable answer. Take the pressure off, plainly. A few won’t take the call, and that’s fine.

STEP 02

Walk backward from the purchase. Work the gaps.

You can’t ask anybody what question they were holding, because nobody knows. Ask directly and you get a tidy reason invented after the fact. People can’t report their past state of mind, but they remember what they did and roughly when. Start at the purchase and ask what they did just before that, and before that, until you reach the week something changed.

Q1 · THE TRIGGER

What was happening in the week you started looking?

Gets you the trigger, and a date you can be present for next time.
Q2 · THE GAP

What did you do next, and why then rather than a month earlier?

Something moved in that gap. Whatever moved is the question that row was holding.
Q3 · THE ROOM

Who else was involved in the decision?

Almost every stall involves somebody who was never in the room with you.
Q4 · THE BELIEF

What would have had to be true for you to sign that week?

“What made you wait” gets a reason. This gets a belief, and a belief is something you can build for.
Avoid: “Why did you choose us?”Gets you a compliment.
Avoid: “What could we improve?”Gets you a feature request.
Avoid: “Were you worried about…?”Tells them what to say.

Notes go in each conversation’s card in step one: open Notes from this conversation. Listen during the call. Write the rows in their words afterward, not during.
